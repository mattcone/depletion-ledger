1. Executive summary and key price drivers 
Total Hormuz Clearance averaged 8,264 kbd over the 7 days to 11 November, about 48% of the pre -war level of 
17,148 kbd. Strait of Hormuz transits made up 5,660 kbd of that average, and Gulf of Oman ship -to-ship net exports 
added 2,604 kbd. Adding Fujairah and Oman terminal loadings lifts the 7-day average to 12,357 kbd, or 60% of the 
pre-war level of 20,508 kbd. Those terminal loadings averaged 4,093 kbd, about 20% above their pre -war level. 
Adding Red Sea volumes gives a regional total of 16,793 kbd, or 72% of the pre-war level of 23,312 kbd. The latest 4 
days are preliminary and have typically settled 1.5 to 2 times higher, so the 7-day average is the better guide. Kpler 
data show Middle East crude and condensate outages of about 3.8 mbd in early November, against about 13 mbd in 
late March.

Table and chart methodology and caveats 
● Every figure is a confirmed floor and can revise upward at any horizon. The recency tiers mark where 
revisions concentrate, not where they stop. 
● Reported figures are older than 14 days, provisional figures are 8 to 14 days old, and preliminary figures 
cover the last 7 days. Preliminary figures have typically settled 1.5 to 2 times higher once confirmations land.  
● Gulf of Oman, Kutch Light and Red Sea volumes with no confirmed load terminal capture ship -to-ship cargo 
net of unmatched arrivals, and they carry no origin attribution. The Iranian-origin exclusion therefore applies to 
strait transits only.

2. Chokepoint and transit metrics 
A. Strait of Hormuz 
Strait of Hormuz transits, excluding Iranian-origin cargoes, averaged 5,660 kbd over the 7 days to 11 November. That 
is about 33% of the pre-war level of 17,148 kbd. Gulf of Oman ship-to-ship net exports averaged 2,604 kbd against a 
pre-war baseline of 0 kbd. Together they gave Total Hormuz Clearance a 7-day average of 8,264 kbd, or 48% of the 
pre-war level. The latest 4 days are preliminary. Total Hormuz Clearance was 1,801 kbd on 8 November, 5,029 kbd 
on 9 November, 4,088 kbd on 10 November and 12,827 kbd on 11 November. The 11 November reading includes 
6,353 kbd of Gulf of Oman ship-to-ship net exports. Such readings have typically settled 1.5 to 2 times higher, 
because each transit is confirmed against more than one source. Adding Fujairah and Oman termi nal loadings lifts the 
7-day average to 12,357 kbd, or 60% of the pre-war level of 20,508 kbd. Adding Red Sea volumes gives 16,793 kbd, 
or 72% of the pre-war level of 23,312 kbd. 
Strait of Hormuz tanker crossings averaged 11 a day over the same 7 days, against 50 before the war. Entries into 
the Mideast Gulf averaged 6 a day and exits averaged 5, each against 25 before the war. The latest day, 11 
November, shows 8 crossings. That is the freshest count. Many transits are dark, so confirmation takes longer and 
the latest count is the most likely to change. Kpler data show Japan is on course to receive 2.81 mbd of crude in 
November, the highest monthly volume since the war began. Supply loaded outside the strait is on course to make up 
54% of those arrivals, against 12% in February. 
B. Bab el-Mandeb and Red Sea 
Red Sea volumes averaged 4,436 kbd over the 7 days to 11 November, about 58% above the pre -war level of 2,804 
kbd. The latest 4 days are preliminary. They were 3,850 kbd on 8 November, 4,867 kbd on 9 November and 5,507 
kbd on 10 November. The reading for 11 November is 0 kbd, which is the freshest and the most likely to change. 
Kpler data show Saudi crude flows through Bab el-Mandeb fell to about 460 kbd in October. Suez Canal transits of 
Saudi crude reached a record 930 kbd, with 62% bound for Europe. Rece ipts at Ain Sukhna along the SUMED 
corridor reached 1.9 mbd.

3. Maritime risk and fleet status 
Kpler recorded 27 liquids crossings of the strait from 8 to 11 November. Of these, 24 were dark or unknown route 
transits and 3 followed Iranian routes. ADNOC Logistics and Services confirmed that a vessel in its fleet was 
damaged in an attack in UAE territorial waters on 10 November. Kpler analysis notes that vessels are reportedly 
coming under attack almost daily in and around the strait. It also notes reports of explosions that halted 2 laden 
supertankers in the strait late on 11 November. Kpler analysis points to the US keeping its blockade on Iran while 
avoiding, for now, another round of direct strikes. The security situation in the Gulf remains highly volatile.

4. Critical infrastructure status 
Saudi infrastructure, Petroline and Yanbu 
Kpler data show Saudi crude exports averaged 7 mbd in October, above the 6.2 mbd pre -war baseline, for a second 
consecutive month. East Coast loadings averaged 3.8 mbd, and West Coast exports held near 3 mbd. Saudi crude 
only output averaged 10.02 mbd in October against 10.1 mbd before the war. Middle East crude and condensate 
outages have fallen to about 3.8 mbd, and Kuwait remains about 660 kbd below its pre -war output. Iran accounts for

5. Refined products and bunker fuel impact 
Crude weakened sharply after the US signalled on 10 November that further attacks on Iran were highly unlikely. 
Brent fell to $91.49/bbl and WTI to $84.86/bbl that day, as trend-following funds sold about 50,000 lots across crude 
and products. Brent turned higher on 12 November after the US said it no longer needed a deal with Iran. A 4.7 mb 
build in US crude stocks capped the bounce. OPEC+ output reached a wartime high of 37.42 mbd in October on 
Kpler estimates, led by a 1.5 mbd recovery in Saudi production. 
Mideast Gulf crude discounts have deepened as Asian refiners turn to Atlantic barrels. Dubai’s prompt premium, 
measured by the M1 to M3 spread, narrowed from $11.03/bbl on 6 October to $1.03/bbl on 12 November. Saudi 
Arabia and Kuwait have cut December official selling prices by $2/bbl. Kpler analysis is slightly bearish on Dubai 
structure, as fears of imminent military escalation recede and Middle Eastern supply rises.  
The sell-off was uneven across products. RBOB fell 6.1% and Singapore fuel oil 6.3%, both faster than crude, while 
gasoil lost only 1.7%, so diesel cracks widened. Russia has extended its export ban through January after Ukrainian 
strikes on its refineries resumed on 30 October. Kpler expects Russian runs to stay constrained around 4 mbd. In 
Singapore, the 95R gasoline crack added $3.6/bbl on the week. Singapore fuel oil lost its prompt tightness as Middle 
Eastern supply picked up. Late October arrivals from the Middle East built high sulfur floating storage volumes. Kpler 
analysis is slightly bullish on Singapore low sulfur fuel oil for November, on prompt demand and inventory draws. 
Middle East refinery runs recovered to 7.7 mbd in October, and Kpler expects a slower rise of about 63 kbd in 
November.

Standing methodology note, vessel and cargo tracking 
Kpler compiles vessel and cargo tracking data from 3 complementary sources. Automatic Identification System (AIS) 
signals form the backbone, providing real-time position data whenever a vessel's transponder is active. A gap in that 
signal is informative but not conclusive on its own. Satellite imagery, both optical and synthetic aperture radar (SAR), 
corroborates vessel presence and laden state at terminals and along transit routes. SAR can see through cloud cover 
where optical imagery cannot. These streams are supplemented by human intelligence, meaning market sources 
reporting loading events, ship-to-ship transfer scheduling and port call activity. This intelligence often flags a 
movement before satellite imagery can confirm it. 
The 3 streams are cross-referenced against a binary confirmation standard. An event is recorded only when 
consistent evidence from more than one source supports it, not through probabilistic attribution or a single source. 
Where AIS coverage lapses, such as during a dark transit, analysts look for satellite imagery placing a vessel inside a 
region on one date and outside it on another. They also draw on human intelligence and discharge -side confirmation 
that corroborates timing and cargo grade. Volumes that cannot be independently verified this way are classified as 
unknown origin rather than attributed by inference, a deliberately conservative approach that favours accuracy over 
completeness.

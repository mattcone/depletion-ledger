1. Executive summary and key price drivers 
Oil flows through the Strait of Hormuz remain well below pre-war levels. Total Hormuz clearance averaged 8,462 kbd 
over the 7 days to 28 October, about 49% of the pre-war baseline of 17,147 kbd. The latest 4 days are preliminary 
floors, ranging from 4,346 kbd on 28 October to 12,202 kbd on 27 October. Such readings have typically settled 1.5 to 
2 times higher, because each transit is confirmed against more than one source. The 7-day average smooths day-to-
day timing noise, but it covers the same recent days that are still preliminary, so it can also revise upward. Bypass 
routes are offsetting part of the loss, with Fujairah and Oman terminal loadings averaging 4,634 kbd over 7 days, 
about 36% above their pre-war baseline. Kpler Insight reports that Saudi East Coast loadings are holding up, with 9 
VLCCs at berth on 28 October. 
Explore these flows in the new Middle East Flows dashboard in the Kpler terminal. It brings Strait of Hormuz transits, 
Gulf of Oman ship-to-ship net exports, Gulf of Oman terminal loadings and Red Sea exports together in one view.

Table and chart methodology and caveats 
● 
Every figure is a confirmed floor and can revise upward at any horizon. The recency tiers mark where 
revisions concentrate, not where they stop. 
● 
Reported figures are older than 14 days, provisional figures are 8 to 14 days old, and preliminary figures 
cover the last 7 days. Preliminary figures have typically settled 1.5 to 2 times higher once confirmations land. 
● 
Gulf of Oman, Kutch Light and Red Sea volumes with no confirmed load terminal capture ship-to-ship cargo 
net of unmatched arrivals, and they carry no origin attribution. The Iranian-origin exclusion therefore applies to 
strait transits only. 
● 
Pre-war baselines are averages over March 2025 to February 2026 on the same definitions and product-
bucket selection. 
● 
Strait crossings are dated by transit, unattributed ship-to-ship cargo by the date it surfaces or departs, and 
berth loadings by loading date. Daily totals therefore mix event timings, and the 7-day averages absorb most 
of that noise. 
Primary drivers 
● 
Clearance through the strait remains about half of the pre-war baseline on a 7-day basis, while Fujairah and 
Oman terminal loadings run above theirs. Section 2 sets out the detail. 
● 
Gulf barrels are finding routes around Hormuz and loosening physical markets, while escalation risk ahead of 
the US midterms on 3 November keeps futures supported. Section 3 covers the risks to Gulf transits. 
● 
Saudi East Coast loadings are recovering but remain well below pre-war levels, and returning Middle Eastern 
barrels are pressuring European sour differentials. Section 4 covers Saudi export routes and grade effects. 
● 
Asia-bound Saudi flows through Bab el-Mandeb have subsided on security concerns, and Egypt’s Sidi Kerir is

●

● 
Refined product cracks are diverging by region, with European gasoline cracks sharply lower and Singapore

2. Chokepoint and transit metrics 
A. Strait of Hormuz 
Strait transits excluding Iranian-origin vessels averaged 7,195 kbd over 7 days, about 42% of the pre-war baseline of 
17,147 kbd. The latest 4 days, all preliminary, ranged from 2,472 kbd on 28 October to 8,918 kbd on 27 October. 
Such readings have typically settled 1.5 to 2 times higher as confirmations land. Gulf of Oman ship-to-ship net exports 
ranged from negative 2,979 kbd on 26 October to 3,284 kbd on 27 October. Net exports turn negative when ship-to-
ship imports exceed exports on the day. Crude averaged 7,290 kbd of clearance over 7 days, well above the 1,172 
kbd of products. Tanker crossings averaged 11 over 7 days, against a pre-war level of 50. The count of 8 on 28 
October is the freshest day. Many transits are dark, so confirmation takes longer and the latest count is the most likely 
to change. 
Bypass routes continue to offset part of the loss. Fujairah and Oman terminal loadings averaged about 36% above the 
pre-war baseline of 3,417 kbd over 7 days. Kpler Insight reports that Basrah exports are back to 3 mbd, about 300 
kbd below pre-war levels, and that Kirkuk loadings from Ceyhan reached 420 kbd in October. Total Iraqi oil exports 
are back to pre-war levels. The UAE has told Asian refiners that Upper Zakum crude will flow through an accelerated 
pipeline to Fujairah in early 2027, which doubles ADNOC’s capacity to bypass Hormuz.

B. Bab el-Mandeb and Red Sea 
The 7-day average of Red Sea volumes, at 3,017 kbd, sits about 8% above the pre-war baseline of 2,804 kbd. The 
latest 4 days, all preliminary, ranged from 771 kbd on 28 October to 7,524 kbd on 25 October. The series covers 
Saudi cargoes and unattributed ship-to-ship volumes with no confirmed load terminal. Daily readings swing widely, 
and ship-to-ship cargo is dated by the day it surfaces or departs, so recent days can revise upward. 
On 29 October, Kpler Insight reported that actualised Saudi West Coast loadings were near 2.6 mbd in October to 
date, about 400 kbd lower than in September. Asia-bound flows through Bab el-Mandeb have subsided on security 
concerns, to about 250 kbd from a peak of about 3.4 mbd in June. Egypt’s Sidi Kerir is now the main bypass route to 
Asia, with loadings near 1.5 mbd. More than half of October Sidi Kerir loadings are expected to reach Asian 
customers, on a voyage of around 55 days via the Cape of Good Hope. 
3. Maritime risk and fleet status 
On 23 October, Kpler Insight cited the IMO as reporting at least 10 attacks on VLCCs in October alone. That is a third 
of the 32 incidents recorded across the conflict. Kpler Insight reports only a slight recovery in clean product flows 
through the Strait of Hormuz and the Gulf of Oman this month, given the attacks on vessels in October. It also reports 
that Tehran proposed shortening Iran’s Hormuz reopening plan to 4 to 5 days if Washington lifts its blockade. Iran has 
threatened legal action against Sinokor from next week, and Sinokor carries nearly 40% of Hormuz crude. Kpler 
Insight says that threat amplifies freight and insurance risks. 
Kpler Insight reports that clean freight from the Mideast Gulf to Japan has reached very high levels. LR2 tankers 
shifting to crude this year have trimmed clean vessel availability. Kpler classed 32 of the 38 oil tanker crossings 
recorded from 25 to 28 October as dark or unknown route.

4. Critical infrastructure status 
Saudi infrastructure, Petroline and Yanbu 
Kpler Insight reports that Saudi crude exports are averaging 6.15 mbd in October to date, against 7.16 mbd in 
September. East Coast loadings of 3.49 mbd have recovered from the mid-year low but remain well below the 5 to 6 
mbd seen before the March 2026 disruption. Juaymah accounts for about 71% of East Coast exports. On 27 October, 
the Houthis claimed a ballistic missile strike on the Ras Tanura refinery. Sentinel-2 imagery from 28 October 
nonetheless shows 9 VLCCs at Juaymah and Ras Tanura, with Ras Tanura at its busiest in loadings since early 
2025. On 22 October, Kpler Insight reported that Aramco’s East-West Pipeline, known as Petroline, resumed 
operations on 22 September after a drone attack on 10 September forced a halt. Aramco continues to offer spot 
cargoes from Yanbu and Sidi Kerir. Kpler Insight says returning Middle Eastern barrels are pressuring sour grades in 
Europe. The Johan Sverdrup premium to Dated Brent has slid to $0.25/bbl, from a record $24/bbl in mid-September. 
The November Dated to Frontline spread has eased to about $5.9/bbl. 
 
Sources, Kpler Insight publications dated 14 September, 22 October, 23 October and 29 October 2026. 
5. Refined products and bunker fuel impact 
Kpler Insight reports that weakening demand and improving supply are pressuring refined products, while geopolitical 
disruptions limit the downside across the barrel. US Gulf Coast gasoline cracks rose by $2.3/bbl and RBOB cracks by 
$3.6/bbl, despite a large sell-off late the week before. US gasoline inventories rose by nearly 4 mb to 209.2 mb, back 
inside the seasonal range for the first time in 25 weeks. In Europe, the Northwest Europe gasoline crack fell by 
$5.3/bbl as maintenance winds down and blending demand dries up. 
France is offering volumes from its strategic reserves as part of a 10 mb release over 3 months from November. 
Russian refinery run rates have improved to an estimated 5 mbd, which should ease values across West of Suez 
markets. More clean exports from Yanbu on LR2 tankers are offsetting the slight recovery in Hormuz flows, with most 
heading to the Mediterranean or Northwest Europe. Clean freight from the Mideast Gulf to Japan has reached very 
high levels, because LR2 tankers shifting to crude this year have trimmed clean vessel availability.

Standing methodology note, vessel and cargo tracking 
Kpler compiles vessel and cargo tracking data from 3 complementary sources. Automatic Identification System (AIS) 
signals form the backbone, providing real-time position data whenever a vessel's transponder is active. A gap in that 
signal is informative but not conclusive on its own. Satellite imagery, both optical and synthetic aperture radar (SAR), 
corroborates vessel presence and laden state at terminals and along transit routes. SAR can see through cloud cover 
where optical imagery cannot. These streams are supplemented by human intelligence, meaning market sources 
reporting loading events, ship-to-ship transfer scheduling and port call activity. This intelligence often flags a 
movement before satellite imagery can confirm it. 
The 3 streams are cross-referenced against a binary confirmation standard. An event is recorded only when 
consistent evidence from more than one source supports it, not through probabilistic attribution or a single source. 
Where AIS coverage lapses, such as during a dark transit, analysts look for satellite imagery placing a vessel inside a 
region on one date and outside it on another. They also draw on human intelligence and discharge-side confirmation 
that corroborates timing and cargo grade. Volumes that cannot be independently verified this way are classified as 
unknown origin rather than attributed by inference, a deliberately conservative approach that favours accuracy over 
completeness.

## 3. Direct comparison with the issued standoff assumption

- **The assumption's premise is working.** The workaround it presupposes is
  demonstrably carrying 8.87 mb/d, +43% above pre-war bypass, absorbing ~4.5
  mb/d of the strait loss. Had the workaround failed, the evidence would be
  pointing TOWARD lapse, not recovery. This supports the mechanism the
  "flat" path assumes.
- **But the momentum is on the recovery side, and it is not a Hormuz story.**
  OPEC September +1.78 (Saudi +2.0), shut-in at its war-low, regional 7-day
  recovering from 15.5M (Sep) to 19.0M (Oct 17), and an unconfirmed
  at/above-prewar regional print — all while the strait sits at −54%. That is
  precisely the case the review names: **Gulf production recovering through
  bypass and reopened loadings while Hormuz remains restricted.** The flat
  assumption excludes exactly this.
- **Nothing yet falsifies the assumption.** October production is unobserved;
  19,041 is still 19% below pre-war and is consistent with "flat production +
  working workaround + some Gulf inventory release" exactly as much as with
  "continued production recovery." The EIA's own view (baseline rising to
  ~111.5 by Dec 2027) is that recovery continues; the standoff is the
  deliberately conservative counter-reading. The evidence has tilted modestly
  toward the EIA side; it has not crossed.
- The stored re-examination condition is **one briefing away** on its
  shipping half: regional ≥19 mb/d is MET by the Oct 19 edition (data Oct 17);
  the ~Oct 26 edition completes the pair if it holds ≥19. The at/above-prewar
  confirmation is the other half. That is a coincidence worth naming: the
  illustrative trigger and the production-path question converge on the same
  next two prints.

- If even half the Saudi September pace (+2.0) persisted into October, OPEC

  clear 101.30 — the flat assumption would be wrong in direction, though the
  magnitude would sit between standoff and endpoint C, not at the EIA path.
  This is a judgment about a recovery pace; the data to test it land ~Nov 10.

- If a named Gulf field/terminal damage is confirmed at any point: the moot

  issued standoff path stands as published, and the damage is assessed on its
  own merits.

## 5. Recommendation

**RETAIN the issued v2026-10-15 standoff path for now.** Reasons:

1. The assumption is a forward judgment about recovery PACE, and every direct
   production observation to date (September) is already inside its baseline.
   No post-issuance production print exists to contradict it.
2. The export evidence is genuinely ambiguous between "flat production + a
   working workaround" (which supports the assumption's own mechanism) and
   "continued recovery through bypass" (which would undermine it). The
   channel that disambiguates — Gulf inventories / October production — is
   unobserved.
3. The tilt is real and should be priced in as process, not as a silent
   re-issue: **pre-commit a dated re-examination at the OPEC November MOMR
   (~Nov 10)**, jointly with the ~Oct 26 Kpler edition and the 24.0M
   confirmation status. Decision rule (stated now, applied later): if **two of
   the three** show recovery beyond September — (a) OPEC October output ≥
   21.49 mb/d, (b) regional sustained ≥19 mb/d OR a comparable-basis
   at/above-prewar confirmation, (c) the EIA/IEA November editions raising
   their Q4-2026 world-production path — then issue a **new dated scenario
   version between standoff and endpoint C** by explicit human decision. If
   OPEC October reverts to flat/declining, retain. This requires no Hormuz
   normalization and no agreement with the full EIA path, per the review.
4. No trajectory is invented here: the possible revision's DIRECTION (toward
   endpoint C, partial) is stated; its numbers await the new dated decision
   and the November prints. The v2026-10-15 records are untouched.

**Not the trigger:** the candidate filter's odds. Its HORIZON responds to
corridor-state evidence (and, per this pass's reproduction, mostly through the
crossings count at high volume — the barrels↔counts relationship remains a
hypothesis); the storage path turns on production and stocks. More responsive
odds alone do not — and must not — update the storage forecast.

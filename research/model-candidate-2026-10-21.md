## 1. Diagnosis: why the shipping evidence barely moves the display

Reproduced read-only from the actual code (diagnosis script:
`research/model/candidate_diagnosis.py`). With transits 12, tanker losses 1,
Brent 108.79, prior 10/50/40 fixed, and only the volume input swept:

| volume (mb/d) | STATE holds / standoff / lapse (unrounded) | displayed HORIZON |
|---|---|---|
| 7.6 (current input) | 2.998e-08 / 99.99997% / 5.884e-09 | 15.0 / 50.0 / 35.0 |
| 10.0 | 1.070e-07 / 100.00000% / 2.301e-10 | 15.0 / 50.0 / 35.0 |
| 13.0 | 3.612e-07 / 100.00000% / 1.038e-11 | 15.0 / 50.0 / 35.0 |
| 17.3 (pre-war scale) | 1.359e-06 / 100.00000% / 3.550e-13 | 15.0 / 50.0 / 35.0 |

The holds-state share rises **45x** (3.0e-08 → 1.36e-06) across the sweep;
the displayed row does not move at all. Four distinct causes, each measured
(ablation-based — a channel's effect depends on the other channels
present, so single-channel densities alone do not rank the forces):

**(1) Price dominance — and it points at lapse.** Ablating one channel at a
time on the full filter: removing **Brent** is the only single removal that
moves the state by orders of magnitude (holds-share 3.0e-08 → 0.413%; a
~1.4e7 difference — the largest single-channel effect in the filter). Run
with the price channel ALONE, at 108.79, it puts 41.5% of the state at
standoff and **58.5% at LAPSE** (HORIZON 9.1/26.6/64.3; normalized pull 64%
lapse / 36% standoff) — the only channel that pulls mostly at closure at
the current data. That is the failure mode the review flagged: high prices
at recovered throughput drag the state toward closure, and "expensive ≈
closing" is not a demonstrated relation. The level is also contaminated as a
current-state observation — it embeds freight (~half the premium per
Kpler), war-risk insurance, gasoil crack tightness ($80-90) and the risk
premium, not only corridor state. And its parameters (N(88,4)/N(97,9)/
N(108,12)) are pure judgment, never fitted.

**(2) The remaining channels jointly pin the state at standoff.** Ablating
transits, volume or tanker losses leaves the state essentially pinned
(99.9988% standoff without transits): at 7.6 mb/d the volume pull is
standoff-leaning (median 8.5) and 12 crossings sits between the holds mode
(λ=20) and the lapse mode (λ=2) with both tails tiny. Note the nuance the
review caught: **transits only** does NOT pin the state — the count
likelihood alone normalizes to ~15.7% holds / ~84.3% standoff / ~0.001%
lapse (before the prior); the near-pinned full-filter state is the product
of the count, price, volume and loss channels together. Nothing in the
current input set points at holds, so a 45x change in the holds share is a
change inside an already-invisible residual.

**(3) The transition matrix compresses the residual.** HORIZON = STATE ×
the fixed judgment rows (holds 60/30/10, standoff 15/50/35, lapse 5/10/85).
With the state ~100% standoff, the HORIZON is ~the standoff row whatever
the residual does. This is the documented judgment table — it is the right
home for *future-deterioration* risk — but it also means the display cannot
reflect current-state movement smaller than the prior mass on the
non-dominant states.

**(4) Stale and overlapping inputs.** `--volume 7.6` is the Oct 14
briefing's print (data to Oct 12); the same-definition successor (Oct 19
briefing, data to Oct 17) is **7.894** — 9 days stale. `--transits 12` is a
3-day average of Oct 5/6/7 daily tanker crossings; the freshest same-series
print is the 7-day average of **10/day** (Oct 17 daily: 6) — ~2 weeks
stale. And transits and volume are the **same underlying quantity**
(corridor function) measured two ways (vessel counts vs barrels). Counts
and barrels can diverge for reasons unrelated to AIS (cargo sizes, load
factors, shuttle geometry), so multiplying both at weight 1.0 is a
plausible double-count — but 0.5 is a hypothesis, not a calibrated joint
likelihood. Note for the record: the briefing's "34 of 43 crossings
dark/unknown-route" (Oct 14-17) does NOT establish a miss rate — those
movements are INCLUDED in the count (Kpler's dark-transit methodology,
satellite + HUMINT against a binary standard); 79% is a route-coverage
statistic. The Sep 21 refactor proposal (`model-refactor-throughput-
signal.md`) intended the transits demoted to 0.5 when the volume channel
was added; the v2.4 implementation kept it at 1.0. Historical author intent
is not calibration.

The price sweep confirms the dominance ranking: in the current model,
Brent 90→150 moves the displayed HORIZON more than any shipping input does
(15.5/49.8/34.7 at 90; 15.0/50.0/35.0 at 108.79; and the STATE lapse share
rises to 1.9e-04 at 150 vs 5.9e-09 at 108.79).

## 3. Candidate design

Three objects, kept separate as the review requires:

| object | where it lives in the candidate |
|---|---|
| current export throughput / recovery | the volume channel (strait transits 7-day avg, lognormal medians 15.0/8.5/2.0, σ 0.35 — unchanged) |
| shipping danger / transport constraint | the tanker-loss channel (Poisson 0.2/1.5/4.0, weight 0.5 — unchanged; input = CONFIRMED tanker LOSSES only — ordinary attacks that leave no confirmed loss are not a filter input) + the demoted transit count as cross-check |
| future deterioration risk | the transition matrix (unchanged judgment table) — plus, outside the filter, the judgment's existing EIA-spread price trigger |

Honesty notes (per the review): (a) the three roles are three CHANNELS on
ONE shared latent state (volume × count × losses are multiplied into a
single state posterior) — they are not three separately estimated
processes; (b) the inherited volume medians are anchored to the pre-v2.4
Kpler crude-era prints (pre-war ~15M) while the input is now the total-oil
strait measure (baseline 17.3) — an acknowledged calibration mismatch
(inherited, already admitted in `branch_filter.py`), which removing the
price channel does not resolve; (c) the candidate's CLI now takes
`--volume-date` / `--transits-date` / `--today` and reports staleness
(14 tests pin it); using the 7-day count average as the `--transits` input
is a window-convention change vs the documented rounded 3-day average, and
the likelihood's sensitivity to the window is NOT tested — flagged, not
resolved.

**Change 1 — the Brent channel is removed from the current-state filter.**
Assessment of where price belongs: *not in current-throughput inference; in
future-risk assessment it already lives (judgment's EIA-spread trigger:
"widening beyond ~$10-15 into October ⇒ the market is pricing lapse > 40%");
it also keeps its role in the draw-rate band via elasticity.* Reasons:
(a) at the current level it is the dominant force and it points at lapse —
"expensive ≈ closing" is not a demonstrated relation; (b) the level embeds
freight/insurance/product tightness, not only corridor state; (c) its
parameters are judgment, never fitted — removing an unfitted, contaminated,
dominant channel is a design correction, not a parameter choice. A reduced
weight was considered and rejected: at 108.79 with weight 0.25 the channel
still puts ~56% of its pull on lapse — the contamination is directional,
not a magnitude problem. No new price "horizon modifier" is added to the
filter: that role is documented as judgment, and adding a numeric modifier
would be new tuning without demonstrated justification.

**Change 2 — the transits Poisson weight is demoted 1.0 → 0.5. A
hypothesis, not a calibrated joint likelihood.** The count and the barrels
are the same underlying quantity measured two ways; low counts at high
barrels can reflect cargo sizes, load factors and shuttle geometry as well
as AIS coverage (the briefing's count INCLUDES satellite/HUMINT-confirmed
dark crossings — 79% dark is route coverage, not a miss rate). 0.5 is not
derived from data: it is the Sep 21 refactor proposal's intended demotion,
re-adopted. A volume-only comparator is in the validation doc. The count
keeps cross-check force at the demoted weight: at 17.3 mb/d, low counts
(4/day) cut the holds state share from 46.6% (14/day) to 2.65% (scenario 7
below).

**Deliberately NOT changed:** prior 10/50/40; volume lognormal family and
vol==0 closure limit; tanker channel; DEFAULT_TRANSITION (the judgment's
table — changing it would be changing the judgment, which is the user's
call, not a filter revision); calibration ledger (the candidate does not
write to it); CLI shape (brent accepted for parity, ignored, reported as
context).

**What was evaluated and rejected:**
- *Retuning the transit λ's (20/10/2):* no — the in-code discipline stands
  (do not re-tune while the count-vs-volume relationship is unresolved).
- *Adding a weekly-attack channel (UKMTO count):* no — ~6 weeks of data, an
  unverified floor, and a misplacement-prone feed (QC Intel's Oct 20
  headline put the Al Faw incident at Duqm). The tanker-loss channel is the
  loss measure; attacks stay in the watchlist until the count is
  primary-verified.
- *A price channel conditioned on volume:* no — it would import the
  same contamination with extra machinery.

## 5. The storage-path connection

The question: what does the shipping evidence support changing in the
forward production assumptions (the issued v2 standoff path: world
production flat at the September reported 101.30 mb/d through 2026, then
non-Gulf growth only — "no net Gulf recovery")?

The evidence, with dates and vintages (see framework section 3):
- the same-definition Kpler regional measure recovered from −28% (Oct 14
  edition) to **−19%** (Oct 19 edition, 19,041 kbd 7-day avg) — a real,
  improving, but partial recovery, on a preliminary floor;
- the East-West bypass ran ~5.8 mbd (Oct 12, industry sources); 42 mb
  west-coast loadings Sep 17–Oct 4; Fujairah/Oman +34%, Red Sea +54% above
  baseline;
- a syndicated print (Bloomberg, Oct 20) claims regional exports
  **fractionally above pre-war** — unreconciled (no definition/window);
- the strait measure (−54%) and the attack rate (18 vessels in 2 weeks)
  argue against "full recovery" while the regional measure argues against
  "no net recovery".

**Assessment: the evidence supports illustrative scenario endpoints, not a
single new path — and not an interval either.** A single candidate number
would require a barrels-exported-to-world-production conversion, which is
not available (exports ≠ production: consumption, stock draws and on-water
effects all sit in between — the September score already measured that:
the 103 mb on-water surge offset most of the 113 mb on-land draw), and
shipment recovery does not identify world production, inventories,
domestic use or a 2027 recovery rate. So, per the review's own gate, the
deliverable is **sensitivity paths with explicit assumptions and an
illustrative re-examination condition** (`research/model/candidate/recovery-path-2026-10-21-candidate.json`).
The endpoints are scenarios (two of them references to issued paths), not
physical bounds, and no supported interval for the eventual outcome is
asserted:

- **endpoint A** = the issued v2 standoff (unchanged, referenced);
- **endpoint B (illustration)** = the month-by-month MIDPOINT between the
  two issued world-production levels at identical consumption (monthly
  adjustment halved: Oct 2026 −0.40 vs the standoff's −0.80, widening to
  Dec 2027 −4.10 vs −8.19). A point on the existing sensitivity line — a
  midpoint of TWO WORLD PRODUCTION LEVELS, not a measured "half-rate Gulf
  recovery"; there is no Gulf/non-Gulf decomposition here, and no
  barrels-exported-to-production conversion is invented;
- **endpoint C** = the EIA baseline itself (v2 corridor-holds, adjustment
  0).

Endpoint-B assumptions (stated, not calibrated): regional 7-day avg holds
at/above 19 mb/d in the next two Kpler briefings; bypass holds at/above
~5.8 mbd; no confirmed Gulf production damage. **No assumption is made
about where the preliminary prints will settle** — the briefing's
typically-1.5-2x statement is Kpler's about the Kpler series, recorded as
provenance and not used to position the midpoint. **Re-examination
condition (ILLUSTRATIVE — not an automatic promotion):** two consecutive
briefings with regional ≥19 mb/d AND a comparable-basis confirmation of
the "at/above prewar" print would argue for re-examining the storage
path; issuing a new dated scenario version requires an explicit, dated
human decision logged in the pass log — the v2026-10-15 records stay
frozen and authoritative until then, and the published dashboard is
unchanged meanwhile. **Moot condition:** confirmed production damage at a
named Gulf field/terminal makes the EIA baseline's corridor-holds premise
itself questionable, so endpoint C (and this illustration built on it) is
no longer a sensible reference; the issued v2 standoff path stands as
published, and the damage would be assessed on its own merits — it does
not by itself restore any prior fixed production level. **Missing inputs
flagged:** the definition/window of the 24.0M print; a same-definition
regional series at the current vintage; the crude/product split on one
basis; bypass sustainability beyond the single 5.8 point.

No issued record, target, anchor or forecast file was modified; the
scoring framework's drift check and selftest both PASS after this pass.

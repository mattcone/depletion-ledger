## Where things stand

Two chokepoints closed (Hormuz + Red Sea/Bab el-Mandeb), a US–Iran tanker war now running on a **published exchange rate** (3 Iranian tankers per 2 warships targeted — 10 Iranian tankers destroyed/disabled in one week, Sep 5–9), Iran's **"exclusion zone" enforced before formally declared** (most strait traffic now rides the Iranian-controlled route; **transits fell to 7 on Sep 9**, down from Kpler's 10–13/day average), and the war's first strike on a third country (20 missiles on the Muwaffaq Salti US base in Jordan — which damaged an A-10 and eight F-15Es, the first US aircraft damage of the exchange). **Sep 10: the heaviest declared shipping wave of the war** (Iran claims 10 ships; UKMTO reports disabling fire on several merchant vessels in the Gulf and Gulf of Oman, a vessel listing off Port Rashid, a ship hit off al-Faw; first seafarer death since the *Sidr*), with the *Andros* — 2M bbl of Iraqi fuel oil — set on fire in **Iraqi territorial waters**. The diplomacy track broke: the **IAEA referred Iran to the UN Security Council 23–3** (first such referral in two decades; Russia/China opposed), and the IRGC's restricted zone got its first geography (Chabahar → parts of the Gulf of Oman — *outside* the strait). The Oman safe corridor is now four days without an IMO/Oman confirmation. Unconfirmed: explosions at a Saudi base (King Fahd) and Taif — if real, first Iranian fire on Saudi soil. The refinery front widened: Houthi strikes re-hit **Aramco's Jazan refinery** (offline since at least late July, nameplate ~400 kb/d), and Ukrainian drones hit the **Novorossiysk export terminal** — the Russian strike campaign now reaches the export side. OPEC+ has paused four consecutive production hikes (Oct flat at 31.01M bpd) — spare capacity is trapped, not withheld. **Sep 11: the Houthis take Mocha, then the entire Yemeni Red Sea coastline** — the second chokepoint's eastern shore is now in Iran-aligned hands; Bab el-Mandeb traffic is unchanged (26 vessels/day). Riyadh called the President twice asking for US strikes on the Houthis and was refused; the acting Navy Secretary separately confirmed the Bahrain base damage.

**Sep 13 (morning): the pause held, the attribution landed, and the ESPO wound got sized.** An Iranian container vessel (10 crew) was struck near Hengam Island — 1 killed, 4 wounded, fire, crew evacuated — and Iran did not answer: four straight days without a launch at a host state, with the Salalah meeting on Monday the most economical explanation. Trump broke the attribution log: "I think they are [behind the pipeline attack]. Probably they are" — the first state attribution by any government, and made by Washington, not Riyadh. Iraq seized the launch platform (al-Tayeb district, Maysan; 800–1,100 km to the targets ⇒ long-range systems) and approved Iran joining the probe. Saudi sources put the ESPO loss at ~700,000 b/d from a damaged pumping station ("at least eight places" per diplomatic channels, unconfirmed; satellite flaring detected; no restart date) — the reversion clause (official assessment of days-scale repair ⇒ back to 10/50/40) is now testable but untriggered. CENTCOM published its blockade math: 100 vessels redirected in 60 days, none through without permission. In Yemen the air war continued (129 strikes claimed/consistent, Marib approaches targeted) and the Houthis began pushing toward Marib — the last major oil-and-gas hub the government holds.

**Sep 12 (evening): the second chokepoint changed hands — and its new masters started an air war.** The Houthis hold the entire Yemeni Red Sea coast, the al-Omari camp overlooking the strait, and Mayyun (Perim) in the middle of the channel; a Yemeni government source: "the Houthis have completed their takeover of the Bab al-Mandeb area." The front is no longer government-vs-Houthi: the Yemeni military confirmed airstrikes on Houthi positions near Mocha and in Taiz, and the Houthis claim 129 Saudi airstrikes in 48 hours (F-15/Typhoon, per Saree; Riyadh has not acknowledged) plus a missile-and-drone strike on the Sharurah base. A Houthi projectile hit Al-Tuwal in Jazan province (2 wounded, a mosque damaged). The Houthis' declared policy — block Saudi vessels, let the rest pass — is now physically enforceable; an analyst puts it as "the real capability to shut down any maritime traffic through Bab el-Mandeb if they want to". Bab el-Mandeb traffic is unchanged (26 vessels/day, Sep 10, last count). Meanwhile the three-day pause in the strait ended (UKMTO: a vessel struck by an unknown projectile, 23:00 UTC Sep 12), and a Tasnim leak says the Iran–Oman agreement is strictly bilateral, sets the basis for reopening but not its timing, runs transit on Iranian protocols, and closes the southern route the US had insisted on.

**Sep 11–12: the bypass is down — and the diplomacy track got a date.** Saudi MoF: the East–West pipeline (Abqaiq–Yanbu, 1,201 km, 7M b/d capacity) was hit by "several drones originating from Iraq" in the Riyadh/Medinah areas; the line is stopped "as a precaution" — no damage assessment, no restart date. Riyadh did not retaliate at the Iraqi PM's request; Iraq's PM office (Sat) confirmed the launch from Maysan province (borders Iran) and dismissed the provincial commander; no claimant, and neither Riyadh nor Baghdad accused Iran. This is a regime event: **judgment was reweighted Sep 11 from 10/50/40 to 10/40/50** (the crisis's first regime-event reweight). The reversion clause — an official ESPO restart, or a damage assessment showing days-scale repair — takes it back to 10/50/40. Meanwhile the diplomacy track materialized: **Gulf foreign ministers + Iran's FM meet in Oman Monday Sep 14** (first Gulf–Iran ministerial of the war); Pezeshkian says the Iran–Oman shared-route agreement is to be signed there and notified to the IMO; FT-cited diplomats: a full reopening still requires a US–Iran understanding, which does not exist. The Houthis completed their Bab el-Mandeb takeover with Mayyun Island (Perim) in the middle of the strait; traffic unchanged (26 vessels/day). A three-day pause in merchant attacks and Iranian launches coincides exactly with the Oman scheduling.

**The scarce asset is product (diesel, jet, East Coast diesel), not crude — and the EIA's own STEO now says so** (distillate crack +11.6%; US distillates forecast below the 5-yr low "through much of 2027"). US retail diesel set a third straight all-time record (**$6.1602, Sep 12 — first above $6.00 on Sep 11**); gasoline $4.3104 (+16¢ in a week) — the political-break tripwire keeps moving further past.

## The base case

**Judgment: 10 / 40 / 50** — unchanged on the Sep 12 evening pass (the Saudi–Houthi air war + Houthi hold on Bab el-Mandeb are intensification of the standoff regime: Red Sea shipping is still moving, 26/day, non-Saudi ships passing). **Two new regime tripwires logged Sep 12:** (a) the Houthis block or attack NON-Saudi vessels in Bab el-Mandeb (or its traffic collapses to the Hormuz pattern) → both chokepoints closed to general shipping = reweight toward lapse; (b) the US strikes the Houthis (MBS asked twice, was refused; CENTCOM commander in-country; 2025 seven-week campaign as precedent) = reweight. — reweighted Sep 11 (evening pass) from 10/50/40 on a regime event: **the East–West pipeline (ESPO) is stopped** with Hormuz in single digits, so the standoff track lost its bypass. Corridor holds: **10%** (the lanes run inside the exclusion zone, mostly Iranian-controlled; the Sep 14 Oman meeting is the only dated reconstitution path, and per FT-cited diplomats it still needs a US–Iran understanding). Standoff: **40%** (both sides' stated rules are being executed rather than announced — a published US exchange rate, an Iranian exclusion zone enforced before formally declared — now without the bypass as its off-ramp). Corridor lapses: **50%.** **Reversion clause: an official ESPO restart, or a damage assessment showing days-scale repair, takes it back to 10/50/40.** The Sep 12 filter (transits 7, tankers 10/wk, settled Brent $104.61, corridor flow absent) says **6.1 / 14.2 / 79.7** — ~30 points more lapse-leaning than judgment; logged, not averaged in. The pre-committed triggers now operate from the 10/40/50 base: the **Sep 16 weekly transit count** sustaining ~7/day (lands with the WPSR) *and* the **EIA–market spread** holding past the **Oct 7 STEO** (now ~$15: EIA 2H26 ~$90 vs settled $104.61 — at the edge of the $10–15 zone), both → further toward lapse.

**The headline is unchanged: the status quo cannot last 12 months.** By month 9, either the corridor or China's buffer breaks — and that break (re-import into a broken supply base, or a 1.2B-bbl release) is the largest market event of the war. The Sep 9 escalation **compresses the dates another ~1–3 weeks**; the sequence is untouched.

**What decides it, in order:** (1) Russia's Sep 30 ban (T-21 — extend into a 17-region shortage or lift it; landing on a base that now includes Novorossiysk, CPC and Ust-Luga); (2) the corridor window — now dominated by the Sep 14 Oman meeting (signing + IMO notification) or the exclusion zone hardening; (3) ARA trader floor + first EU price caps (late Oct–Nov); (4) SPR at 250M (late Oct–Nov, pace-dependent); (5) Russia jet ban (Nov 30). Plus the one undated risk: a calibrated Russia→Europe hybrid attack that fails to fail.

**Even the good case is an 18-month recovery** (ADNOC: full Middle East flow unlikely before late 2027; EIA now says Middle East below pre-conflict output until 2Q27). And the baseline itself is damaged — reopening does not restore pre-war supply (Qatar LNG −17% until 2029–31; crude −0.6M bpd structural, compounding to 1.5–2.5M with drift).

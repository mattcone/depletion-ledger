## Model: unchanged output is not a successful test of recovery

Three distinct objects must not be conflated:

1. Published judgment probabilities: 10/50/40 (research/MODEL.md). These are author judgments under event rules, not automatically replaced by the filter.
2. Diagnostic branch filter: research/model/branch_filter.py, current state nearly 100% standoff, HORIZON rounded to 15/50/35. It is a likelihood calculation with judgment parameters, not an empirically validated causal supply model.
3. Storage paths: frozen v2 production/consumption assumptions, site/src/data/world-stocks-assumptions-v2026-10-15.json. Standoff fixes world production at September’s 101.30 mb/d through December 2026, then adds 2.0 mb/d non-Gulf growth over 2027. Daily shipping inputs do not enter these balances. Changing odds alone cannot change a conditional path.

Not literally identical internally: at 17.3 the holds state share is ~0.000136%, versus ~0.000003% at 7.6, but display rounding conceals the tiny change. At 17.3, HORIZON holds is about 15.00006%. 17.3/20 are stress-test inputs, not verified new same-definition observations and not regional-total replacements.

The main suppressor is the **price likelihood**: holds Normal(mean 88, sd 4); standoff Normal(mean 97, sd 9). At 108.79, price alone favors standoff over holds by ~138,417:1. Re-running at hypothetical volume 17.3 without the price signal gives state ~15.83% holds / 84.17% standoff, showing the other signals/prior also lean standoff. At hypothetical Brent 88 with the other signals fixed and volume 17.3, HORIZON becomes ~33.49/41.78/24.73. These are parameter diagnostics, not recommended odds.

The near-certain standoff state maps to the **preselected transition row [0.15,0.50,0.35]**. This is why the displayed HORIZON repeats. It is not proof that observed recovery leaves future risk unchanged, and it is not a literal hard-coded output or general floor. Because high prices may also reflect freight, insurance and refinery constraints, treating them as overwhelming evidence against a functioning export system needs an empirical justification. This is a model-design concern, not an arithmetic bug discovered in this pass.

Current inputs also repeat older 7.6 volume / 12 transit observations while newer reports are parked for reconciliation. The model therefore has BOTH a stale-input problem and a sensitivity problem; changing 7.6 alone does not resolve the latter. Published judgment odds and fixed storage paths add two further manual decision layers.

### Recommendation, without silently revising a forecast

- Reconcile and date one primary Hormuz series and a separate regional-export series now; publish scope/window/baseline and revisions. Do not substitute 24 mb/d regional exports into the strait-input field.
- Review the production assumption separately from shipping safety. Sustained recovery through bypasses can invalidate “no further Gulf recovery” while attacks persist. Exports alone do not measure production, consumption or stock changes.
- Require sensitivity diagnostics for price, counts and volume plus a reasoned transition table before presenting unchanged filter output as reassurance. Do not merely retune until a preferred answer appears.
- Keep issued forecasts intact for scoring; any adopted recovery revision needs a new dated version. Existing midpoint sensitivity already demonstrates that production recovery can reverse the 2027 direction; it does not estimate the most likely path.

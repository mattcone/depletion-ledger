### Evidence

- **Wall Street Journal, "Saudi Arabia Begins Exporting Oil Via Pipeline
  Damaged in War," Sep 28** (two sources familiar with the matter): Aramco
  resumed pumping crude through the East–West pipeline to Yanbu and has
  **resumed loading tankers there, starting Sunday** — ending the halt that
  cut off the ~4M bpd the line carried before the Sep 10 drone strike.
  Throughput **~3.5M bpd** (≈87% of pre-strike; capacity 7M). Ras Tanura
  exports surged to **~6.5M bpd** (from 1.5M), the highest in weeks; Red
  Sea loadings remain low but are rising; total Saudi crude exports ~7M
  bpd, still below the pre-crisis ~11M. Aramco and ESPO declined to comment.
- **Corroboration:** Dow Jones (marketscreener, Sep 28) — ESPO loadings
  resumed at Yanbu, throughput ~3.5M bpd, near the ~4M pre-strike; Ras
  Tanura ~6.5M bpd; total ~7M bpd, below ~11M pre-crisis. Consistent with
  the Sep 22 Reuters restart wire (low rate, 4M target) and the Sep 28
  Bloomberg resumption.
- **Implication:** this closes the "throughput ≠ loadings" gap that held
  corridor-flow at `degraded` all month — the pipeline is pumping AND the
  tankers are loading.

### External review — methodological corrections (absorbed)

The external review flagged that the model page frames the branch filter as
a data-derived point estimate, which overstates it. Corrected:

1. **The filter is the judgment's own table, not an independent estimate.**
   The "horizon" (15/50/35) is the judgment's own pre-committed standoff row
   (the second row of DEFAULT_TRANSITION). The filter's independent
   contribution is the *state* classification (data-informed, not
   calibrated) and the *direction*/consistency check (it independently
   flagged the Sep 21 divergence). Its precision is the judgment's; it
   should not be read as a data-derived point estimate.
2. **The transit baseline is ~85/day AIS-only, not 125.** The ~125 figure
   is Kpler/Reuters TOTAL large commercial vessels/day; the ~85 figure is
   the AIS-only count (PortWatch) — the method the current daily counts
   use. The "19 tankers/week vs 125/day" framing was apples-to-oranges and
   is retracted; the fair comparison is ~85 (AIS-only) vs the current
   ~9/day (AIS-only).
3. **v2.3 sourced the structural label; it did not neutralize the
   AIS-dark bias.** Volume is a regime-level signal (corridor degraded or
   functioning), not a per-vessel correction. The transit Poisson remains
   the load-bearing state signal.
